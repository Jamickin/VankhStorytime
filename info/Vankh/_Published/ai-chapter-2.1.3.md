# Chapter 2.1.3: Gress

"As above, so below; as below, so above."

---

She found him on the neutral concourse, between the blue corporate section and the patchwork independent quarter where the maintenance corridors surfaced into the public arteries of [[Nexus Station]]. He was standing still with his tool bag over one shoulder, watching the crowd move in its two incompatible streams — the augmented and the un — and not yet going anywhere in particular.

"Specialist P-R-0-4-7-9."

[[Gress]] had changed out of the transit clothes and into something that still read as corporate but carried the particular kind of crispness that came from deliberate effort rather than habit. She had the [[Cicillian Industries]] lanyard badge visible, and she was the only person on the concourse who looked as though she had somewhere specific to be and had chosen to be here instead.

"Stationary Specialist." He adjusted the tool bag strap. "You found your footing."

"I used the [[Cicillian]] contractor registry." She said it without apology, which told him she knew what it meant to track someone down that way and had decided the information she wanted was worth whatever he made of it. "You're listed as a drive coupling specialist, independent contract review, [[Nexus Station]] transit work. The filing was current."

"They keep my filings current." He glanced at the flow of people around them — the neutral concourse was the place you came when you needed to stand somewhere neither side would record. "What did you want to know?"

She was quiet for a moment, the way a person is quiet when they have been preparing what to say and have now decided the prepared version won't serve. "You found something in the drive readings during the transit. I could see it in the way you closed the display."

He looked at her.

"You weren't startled," she continued. "Startled is fast. You closed the display slowly, like you were making a decision about what to do with what you'd just seen. And then you were very careful not to look at the readout again." She held his gaze with the steadiness of someone who spent her professional life communicating through numbers and had learned that steadiness in a speaker generally means the numbers are real. "You found something that surprised you."

"You're a Stationary Specialist," he said. "Resource logistics."

"Resource logistics for a mid-level [[Cicillian Industries]] administrative division. Allocation figures, supply projections, distribution modeling. I have been doing it for nine Vankh-Setts." A pause. "That is what I want to talk to you about."

They walked. It was better to walk on the neutral concourse than to stand — standing drew the kind of attention that registered, while two people moving at a moderate pace registered as transit.

[[Pro]] told her just enough: the harmonics were too precise. In transit, independent vessels produced a characteristic signature — their couplings fought the [[Vankh pulse]] in the particular way that their maintenance history and their component age and their operational load determined, and the fight was always visible as harmonic irregularity, a mechanical argument between the vessel and the physics it was violating. This transit had shown none of that. The coupling's harmonics had been exact to a degree that no mechanical system achieved without external calibration. Natural systems — even well-maintained ones — don't produce perfect values. That was the definition of a natural system. The readings had given him a perfect value.

[[Gress]] listened with the kind of attention that processes quickly and files neatly and does not require him to repeat himself.

"The allocation figures," she said, when he stopped.

"Tell me."

"Resource distribution in my division covers eleven habitat zones. I run monthly projection models against actual consumption — standard audit function, I've done it since my third posting." She was watching the concourse now rather than him, which was its own kind of signal: she had decided the information he needed was in her thinking, not in his face. "Three Vankh-Setts ago I flagged an anomaly in the distribution curves. The curves were too smooth. Actual consumption data never produces smooth curves — there are local events, supply chain delays, seasonal variation in demand patterns, transport failures. The curves account for all of that, and they're still irregular. That's how you know they're real."

"But yours were smooth."

"My curves have been smooth for as long as I have records to check, which is six Vankh-Setts. Resources flowing to destinations in quantities those destinations cannot justify by their population figures or their industrial output. Perfect distribution. No variation that exceeds the model's prediction by more than point-two percent." She turned her head back toward him. "I reported it as a modeling error. I told myself the algorithm was compensating for something I wasn't accounting for. I requested a review from the division's statistical team and they confirmed the model was functioning correctly."

"Which means the model was producing exactly what someone wanted it to produce."

"That is what I have stopped assuming it doesn't mean."

The concourse curved ahead of them, opening toward the junction where the blue section began its gradient in the station's surface layer — the visible shift from neutral to corporate that happened in the lighting before it happened in the architecture.

"Where are you going next?" she asked.

He mentioned [[Helix Habitat]] eventually. Not the whole of it — not his parents, not the medicine route, not the archive chip in his tool bag whose contents he had not yet finished processing. Just the destination and the loose timeline.

[[Gress]] went quiet.

The quiet was not the quiet of someone who had nothing to say. It was the quiet of someone who had decided, in the moment, that what they had to say required more privacy or more time or more certainty than this particular stretch of neutral concourse permitted. He did not press it. He had developed a working relationship with what people chose not to tell him directly.

She said: "When you come back from [[Helix Habitat]]. Tell me if what you find there changes what you think the allocation data means."

She produced a contact code — not spoken aloud, not on a device he would have to carry, but transferred directly to his neural interface through the standard credential-exchange protocol that [[Cicillian]] employees used for internal networking. The transfer logged as professional contact exchange, which was exactly what it was.

"Stationary Specialist."

"Specialist P-R-0-4-7-9." She gave him the particular nod that corporate professionals used when they had reached the functional conclusion of a meeting, and walked back into the blue section of the station, which received her and closed around her in the way that all environments received the people who belonged in them.

[[Pro]] stood on the neutral concourse and did not move for a moment.

The allocation curves. The drive harmonics. Two separate systems, two separate specialists, both running anomalies that resolved the same way when you held them next to each other: a distribution too smooth to be real, harmonics too precise to be mechanical. Something producing, in two entirely different registers, the same quality of excessive correctness. A number that should wobble, held flat. A system that should vary, maintaining a perfect curve.

He was a coupling specialist. He spent his professional life distinguishing between a system that was malfunctioning and a system that was behaving according to a design he had not yet located.

He knew which of those two things this was.

He began walking toward the maintenance corridor that would take him to Berth 47-C, and carried the contact code and the question about [[Helix Habitat]] and the smooth distribution curves alongside the hull scarring and the drive harmonics and his mother's archive, all of them parts of the same thing, none of them yet enough to name it.
