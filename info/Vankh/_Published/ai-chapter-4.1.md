# Chapter 4.1: The Assignment

"Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause; everything happens according to law; chance is but a name for law not recognized."

---

CCLLIAN-ROUTE-9-ALPHA had been commissioned as an independent-class transport seven Vankh-Setts ago, which explained why it operated outside the standard corporate manifest system while carrying corporate route designations. A vessel that could be sent where the official routes didn't acknowledge. [[Pro]] recognized the configuration from his independent contractor years: plausible separation from institutional accountability, deniable as corporate when convenient, accessible as corporate when needed.

He arrived at Docking Bay Thirty-Three early. Not from eagerness — from the habit of arriving before the variables were set, while he still had time to read the room.

The transport was a mid-capacity vessel, twelve seats in two configurations: four in the forward technical section where his assignment placed him, eight in the aft compartment separated by a bulkhead door. The forward section held nothing except his technical observer console and the standard documentation equipment [[Lin]]'s assignment had specified. The aft compartment held the other passengers.

He was the last to board. They were already seated when the forward bulkhead door gave him a partial view through the viewport before it locked — eight passengers in the plain, unmarked clothing of travelers who had made a decision about what they wore that morning. No augmentation ports. No visible [[Cicillian Industries]] credentials. No [[Purist]] markings, though the absence of augmentation and the specific angle of their breathing — deliberate, measured, rhythmically out of phase with the [[Vankh pulse]] — put them in territory the Church occupied without quite naming.

They were not the non-specialist corporate passengers [[Lin]]'s manifest described.

[[Pro]] took the technical observer position and opened the documentation console and did what he did when he needed to think without appearing to think: started checking systems.

The drive coupling was within specification.

The navigation system was within specification.

The logged destination was Relay Station One, Sector 7. The drive harmonics were configured for direct approach. Not a docking approach — not the deceleration profile that Relay Station One's docking hardware required, the approach geometry that all approach geometry to any station required. A trajectory. A heading at full transit velocity aimed at the coordinates on the other side of Relay Station One's docking arm, which was also the coordinates of the third [[Vankh spheres|Vankh sphere]] at seventeen kilometers' distance from the station, which was also the coordinates of a collision.

He ran the calculation three times. Then once more, using the non-standard diagnostic equipment rather than the console, because he needed to be certain he was not misreading the corporate system.

He was not misreading it.

Four Vankh-pulses. At current velocity, four pulses — twelve seconds of real time, experienced at whatever subjective dilation [[FTL travel]] imposed — before the third Vankh sphere and CCLLIAN-ROUTE-9-ALPHA occupied the same location.

[[Pro]] sat with this for one full Vankh-pulse.

VVVVVANKH.

He thought about [[Lin]]'s office. The flat, resolved enhancement patterns. The specific phrasing: *consider it recognition for your Sector 7 work.* The manifest that described eight non-specialist passengers as a "transit evaluation." The medical documentation she had suggested he file. The data she had intercepted and then held without processing through the security review she had described.

He thought about Supervisor L-I-N-3-8-2, who had been everywhere independent contractors worked, who had documented every off-books operation in the sector across three cycles, who had assigned herself to oversight of his maintenance work from the first moment his harmonic readings had diverged from standard. Who found security in a system that required its problems to be solved, and who had now, precisely and efficiently, solved him.

He thought about the eight passengers in the aft compartment breathing out of phase with the pulse — aspirants, the Church called the ones who had moved beyond theology into action. The ones for whom a collision with the source of all impurity was a destination, not an accident.

The secondary harmonic was present. Very close. Closer than it had been in his quarters on Nexus Station, closer than the aperture on the Helix shuttle, close in the way that the thing at the Relay Station viewport had been close — not a matter of physical distance but of attention, of something being present to it.

The eight passengers in the aft compartment did not know what they were carrying forward. They knew only what the Church had given them: a destination and a meaning. They had not chosen this vessel. Someone had chosen it for them.

The same person who had chosen it for [[Pro]].

He looked at his maintenance cloth, spread on the console beside him. The non-standard tools. Eleven years of small improvements, enhanced sensitivity, broader spectrum, pattern-recognition tuned for configurations no corporate manual had described and no corporate manual had needed to suppress, because the only reason to suppress a tool was if someone knew what it might find.

He had found it.

He pulled the third breathing sequence from the deep part of his memory where he had not quite examined it since his mother had shown him — not in words, but in the rhythm of her own breathing during one of the clear-eyed moments that her temporal stabilizers created. Not standard [[FTL travel|FTL breathing]], not the reverse proximity protocol, not either of the two techniques he had been trained in by the Academy or certified in by [[Cicillian Industries]]. The third one. The one that was on no corporate documentation because Cicillian had not invented it and the Church did not use it and it existed only in the archival memory his mother had preserved against all institutional pressure to let it go.

*Left lung. Right lung. Left lung. Right lung. Left lung.*

Even. Both sides. In phase with the [[Vankh pulse]] rather than synchronized to it or counter-phased against it — present to it the way he had finally managed to be present to the secondary harmonic on the shuttle from the Helix, before the aperture collapsed and normal space returned.

The secondary harmonic did not shift the way it shifted when he reached for the scanner. It came nearer in the way that a quiet frequency comes nearer when the room stops competing with it.

VVVVVANKH.

Three pulses left.

[[Pro]] held the even breathing and directed his attention at the third Vankh sphere in the darkness ahead of the vessel and thought: *I know you're there. I've known since the shuttle. My mother knew before that. Whatever the figures in the coupling bay carried for you — I received it.*

The secondary harmonic was, for the first time in his life, fully present.

Not answering. Present. The way a very old and very patient thing is present when someone finally comes to where it has been waiting.

Two pulses.

The aft compartment was completely quiet.
