# Chapter 1.2.3: The Reports

"Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall; the pendulum swing manifests in everything."

---

The data chip was lighter than it had any right to be.

[[Pro]] found an unoccupied terminal in the blue concourse — a documentation station meant for contractors filing maintenance records remotely, tucked into a recess between a commercial kiosk and the atmospheric intake manifold. He had used it twice before, for legitimate paperwork. He sat down, fed [[Augi]]'s chip into the secondary port, and let the compatibility protocol run.

Three reports. Six reference numbers with no data behind them. Five numbers with no archive record at all.

He opened the first accessible report.

The designation at the top was standard [[Cicillian Industries]] format — an ID number, a date, a proximity classification. No name. The incident occurred eleven [[Vankh-Sett]]s ago, Relay Station Three, standard maintenance rotation within seventeen kilometers. The technician had been calibrating a junction coupling on the station's external array and had used the standard [[FTL travel]] breathing pattern by accident — muscle memory, he had written. Eight years of transit work. His hand was on the coupling and the rhythm settled in before he caught it.

The incident itself was two paragraphs.

*At approximately the third breath of the standard pattern I became aware of something I cannot describe as visual, auditory, or tactile, because it was none of these precisely. The nearest I can render it: I became aware of being attended to. Not observed in the way that monitoring equipment observes — there is no weight to that. This carried weight. The sense of an attention so old and so particular that its specificity was itself the message. There was nothing frightening in it. There was nothing comforting in it either. It was simply present in the way that a massive object is present — by the fact that space bends around it.*

*I shifted to the reverse protocol and it ceased. I repeated the standard pattern briefly and it returned. I shifted back. I logged the incident as equipment anomaly and filed this supplementary note through the informal channel. I have not used the standard pattern near a sphere since.*

[[Pro]] read it twice. His hands were resting on the desk, loose. He read it a third time with the part of his mind that assessed coupling data — present to it, not drawing conclusions before the data had finished speaking.

He opened the second report.

Six [[Vankh-Sett]]s ago. A technician at Relay Station One — the oldest installation. She had not been calibrating; she had been performing a structural inspection of the observation blister, and had drifted into the standard rhythm during the work, the way long-practiced patterns occupied the body when conscious attention was elsewhere.

Her description was different in its imagery and identical in its structure.

*Something was paying attention to the same thing I was paying attention to. This is the only way I can say it that doesn't distort it. I was looking at the sphere. The sphere was not looking at me — the sphere is an object. But something in the register I could reach only through the standard pattern was observing the sphere from the same direction I was, and was aware that I was there, and the awareness was precise. It knew the difference between me and the instruments I was carrying. It was not interested in the instruments.*

She had switched protocols, logged the event as a dilation precursor response, and requested reassignment to non-proximity work. Corporate had granted it without review.

The third report was older — nineteen [[Vankh-Sett]]s back, filed through a channel that the archive had partially absorbed into its standard structure, which was why it remained accessible at all. A maintenance engineer on a vessel running a cargo route that passed within seventeen kilometers of the second sphere. The sphere was not the destination, just a point of passage.

This one was shorter.

*I want to note for the record, because I believe records should contain what actually happened and not what we prefer to have happened, that for approximately thirty seconds during the third Vankh-Sett of our closest approach I stopped using the prescribed counter-rhythm and returned to standard breathing. During those thirty seconds I was absolutely and completely certain that I had been recognised. Not encountered — recognised. As if whatever was behind the secondary harmonic had been waiting specifically for me to stop filtering it out, and the moment I did, it acknowledged that it had been expecting this.*

*I have tried to determine whether this experience was hallucinatory. I find I cannot. The phenomenology is consistent with acute sensory event rather than cognitive disruption. I am filing this note because I do not know what else to do with it, and because I believe it matters that someone in the record knows it occurred.*

*If another technician encounters this and knows what it is: please contact me.*

The file had no contact information. Whatever the engineer had meant to be preserved, the contact channel had been removed from the accessible portion of the document. The rest — six reports classified above his clearance, five whose existence was logged but whose content had been entirely removed from the system — told him something about how seriously someone had taken his belief that it mattered.

[[Pro]] pulled the chip from the terminal and sat for a moment in the blue concourse. Corporate citizens reading their overlay data, independent contractors filing manifests, the whole coordinated flow of a station that functioned because everyone in it was attending to the right frequencies at the right times.

*It knew the difference between me and the instruments I was carrying. It was not interested in the instruments.*

He had spent eleven years building instruments of increasing precision. He had been, until the transit from this station, certain that the instruments were the point — that the data they produced was what mattered, and that his own senses were merely the less precise version of the same function.

He filed that away beside the hull scarring and the drive harmonics and the pharmaceutical pack in his inner pocket.

He did not know yet what it meant to be the instrument that mattered.

But fourteen reports — three accessible, six classified, five gone entirely — described the same experience across eleven [[Vankh-Sett]]s and at least three separate proximity sites. He was a man who spent his professional life distinguishing between random variation and a pattern that held because it had been designed to hold.

He knew which of those two things this was.

He gathered his tool bag and moved toward the concourse exit, carrying Augi's chip and three descriptions of a very old and very specific attention, and the growing awareness that it had been waiting for someone to come within range and stop filtering it out.

He had not yet stopped.

He was beginning to understand that this was a choice, and not a permanent one.

The concourse noise reassembled itself around him — announcements, footsteps, the low harmonic of the station's environmental systems. He walked through it.

Somewhere between the documentation terminal and the outer corridor, the reports stopped being data. He kept checking. He kept finding the same attention looking back. He couldn't remember when he had stopped moving.

*Pro has lost his mind.*
