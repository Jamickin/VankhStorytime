# Chapter 3.1.2: The Ceremony

"Everything is dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites; like and unlike are the same."

---

The decree came through on the internal announcement system at the fourth Vankh-Sett: all residents to the central atrium for Rhythm Communion, attendance confirmed by identity wand at each entrance threshold. Mandatory by Church order.

[[Pro]] was still in the family quarters.

He had stayed longer than he planned, the way you stay when you are unsure whether you are delaying the right thing or the wrong one, sitting with his father's silence and his mother's quiet sleeping breath and the weight of what had happened in the shuttle on the way in — the secondary harmonic shifting not to the instrument but to the mind behind it. His father had left for the maintenance level an hour before the decree. His mother had not woken since mid-morning.

He had been calculating the window for a quiet departure when the announcement locked it.

The Church enforcement officers were already in the corridors when he stepped out — not enforcing yet, just present at the junctions, the identity wands ready, their posture carrying the particular quality of authority that does not need to be exercised because the option of not exercising it is the exercise. Two of them at the corridor junction between the family quarters and the transit route to the disembarkation hub. He made a single assessment: departing now meant the wand, meant a delay, meant a query about where he was going and why he was going there during a mandatory gathering, meant precisely the kind of documentation he had spent three years of [[Cicillian Industries]] work learning to avoid generating unnecessarily.

He went to the atrium.

The central atrium of [[Helix Habitat]] had been built during the Cicillian administration as a common space — a pressurized garden volume under a segmented viewport that showed the stellar body the ring orbited, designed for recreation and the kind of inter-section social interaction that corporate productivity models had determined increased retention rates among long-term residents. The Church had kept the viewport and replaced the garden with stone-flagged flooring and symmetrical seating and, at the chamber's far end, a carved representation of the standing human form with its augmentation ports sealed and its hands open: the Prophet's image of humanity returned to its correct state.

The atrium held perhaps four hundred people when [[Pro]] entered. More were filing in behind him. The announcement's window was precise — the kind of precision that came from having run this before, knowing how long it took to fill the space, setting the mandatory window accordingly. He found a position toward the center-rear, equidistant from the nearest two exits, and settled.

VVVVVANKH.

The [[Vankh pulse]] ran through the atrium's stone flooring with a different quality than it ran through the shuttle or the maintenance bays or his quarters on [[Nexus Station]]. The stone was better at conducting it. Or it was the mass of bodies — the particular propagation effect of several hundred people in a pressurized volume, each carrying the pulse in their own chest cavity, all receiving it simultaneously. He had noticed this before at large gatherings and thought of it as an acoustic fact. He was noticing it differently now.

The internal speakers opened. The Prophet's address — not live, recorded decades ago and preserved for use at gatherings like this one — filled the atrium with a voice that had learned, over whatever years of practice, to modulate itself against the [[Vankh pulse]] rather than competing with it. The rhythm of the words fell in counter-phase. Not quite the reverse breathing, but built on the same structural principle: set your signal against the rhythm, occupy the negative space, let the contrast do the work.

*The flesh that has been altered is the flesh that cannot hear the true Rhythm. The Rhythm speaks to what is natural, what is original, what has not been made to serve a function by those who would harvest the sacred for commerce. If you have been made, you are not less. You are waiting. The purification is not punishment — it is return.*

The congregation's response was not uniform, which was the first thing he noted with any specificity.

There were people here who had been attending this ceremony for years. The quality of their stillness was the stillness of long practice — not the tension of someone sitting through something, but the release of someone who has arrived somewhere familiar. Their breathing, he noticed, was already in counter-phase. They did not need to be told. The Rhythm Communion had given them a frame and they had inhabited it.

There were people who were attending because the decree gave them no choice, and who had arrived with the controlled expressionlessness of people performing a civic minimum. He recognized this because he was one of them, and because he had enough practice with controlled expressionlessness to read it in others.

And there were a small number of people who were watching the exits.

Not with panic. Not with the look of someone about to move. With the quiet, peripheral attention of people who know where the exits are and want to keep knowing, the way his father watched corridors, the way the contact on the maintenance level had watched the access panel. The way [[Pro]] himself was watching the atrium right now.

He did not look directly at any of them. Direct attention at this kind of gathering was its own announcement.

The testimonials began after the address. Three residents of the [[Helix Habitat]], speaking at the front of the atrium from behind the carved Prophet's image, describing what the purification had given them: the silence after the implants were removed, the clarity of hearing the Rhythm without technological mediation, the sense of having been returned to something they had not known they were separated from. Their voices carried conviction. He listened to the specific register of conviction, which was different from performance and different from rote repetition, and found himself believing that they were not lying about what they had experienced.

Which was the thing that settled in him during the third testimonial, quietly and without drama, the way the secondary harmonic was present beneath the [[Vankh pulse]] — not announced, just there if you were attending to it.

The [[Church of Purity]] did not remove augmentations to harm people. It removed them because it had a theory about what augmentations were doing, and the theory had a premise — the premise that augmented flesh could not hear the Rhythm — and the premise was wrong, but the experience of the people who had lost their augmentations and then felt something was real. They had lost the instrument. What they heard afterward in the absence of the instrument was whatever was present in the space the instrument had been occupying, which was not silence, which was the same frequency the instrument had been measuring from a different position.

[[Cicillian Industries]] sold augmentations because it had a theory about what augmentations could do for human productivity, and the theory had a premise — the premise that the [[Vankh pulse]] was a resource, a physical phenomenon to be exploited — and that premise was also wrong, but the people who had been augmented and then found they could work faster and process more and perceive at greater resolution were also not reporting an experience that was false.

Two systems of control. Two opposite theories about the same phenomenon. Two incompatible institutional structures, each harvesting the compliance of their populations from opposite directions — one through the promise of enhancement, one through the fear of it. Both had constructed the terms of the question so that the question itself could not be asked: not *what is the Rhythm and what generates it and why*, but only *should you be enhanced or purified*, which was not a question about the Rhythm at all but about which institution you were going to give your life to.

He thought about his father's sealed container in the wall cavity. The people in the maintenance passages below. The Church and [[Cicillian Industries]] had been dividing the [[Helix Habitat]] between them for decades, and the people his father was moving through the conduit system were not choosing between them. They were choosing neither.

That was, perhaps, the more dangerous choice.

VVVVVANKH.

The gathering released forty minutes after it had begun, the exits cleared by Church enforcement in orderly sequence, identity wands running as each resident passed through the threshold. [[Pro]] moved with the crowd's current, taking no particular position in the line, arriving at the threshold when he arrived.

The officer at the exit ran the wand over him with the automatic efficiency of someone who had been doing this for hours. The wand swept his credential chip, his Cicillian documentation, the enhancement inventory he had recited at the disembarkation checkpoint that morning.

The officer paused.

The readout held for three seconds. Four. The officer's eyes moved from the display to [[Pro]]'s face and stayed there with a quality that was not quite suspicion and not quite recognition but somewhere in the narrow territory between them.

[[Pro]] held himself at technical neutral. Not the stiffness of someone managing a reaction. The genuine calm of a man who had been trained by eleven years of proximity work to stay present to what was actually occurring rather than what he feared was occurring, because you could not read a coupling's true state if you were already imagining its failure.

The officer looked back at the readout. Something in his posture made a small, invisible decision.

He let [[Pro]] through.

He said nothing.

[[Pro]] walked the corridor back toward the disembarkation hub with the steady pace of someone who belonged there, and did not look back, and thought about the pause.

He did not know what the readout had shown. He did not know whether the officer had read something that required a decision, or found something routine that had simply taken a moment to process, or recognized something about [[Pro]] that had nothing to do with his documentation. He might never know. The spaces between what people registered and what they chose to do with it were not always legible, even from the inside.

What he knew was that he had been seen — really seen, in the way that the secondary harmonic had been seen by the thing at the aperture that looked back — and that the seeing had not produced a specific outcome, and that the absence of a specific outcome was not the same as safety.

He boarded the shuttle. The transit aperture opened. [[Helix Habitat]] fell away behind him in the dark of the outer ring, carrying its four hundred residents and its mandatory gatherings and its hidden passages and its two institutions competing to name what the Rhythm was and neither of them right, all of it receding to the size of a structure you could cover with your thumb from the forward viewport if you were inclined to do that kind of thing, which [[Pro]] was not.

He held his hands folded in his lap and did not reach for the scanner.
