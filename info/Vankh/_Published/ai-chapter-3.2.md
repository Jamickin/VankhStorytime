# Chapter 3.2: The Crystal

"Everything is dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites; like and unlike are the same."

---

[[Pro]] had been trying to do what his mother said for three cycles, and he had not managed it once.

Every time he directed his attention at the secondary harmonic, he reached for an instrument first. It was a reflex so deeply embedded in eleven years of drive work that he could not separate the act of observation from the act of measurement. He would sit in his quarters in the late Vankh-cycles with his scanner powered down, trying to hold the quality of attention his mother had described — present to it, available, calibrated — and the harmonic would be there, just below the threshold where he had words for it, and then his hand would be on the scanner housing and he would not remember reaching.

He examined the [[data crystal]] instead.

She had pressed it into his hands with the urgency of someone transferring something that needed to be held safely, not understood immediately. He had been carrying it across two Parts of the story without opening it — treating it like the kind of instrument he was not ready to read. That reluctance, he was beginning to understand, was its own kind of answer to a question he had not quite asked.

The crystal's archive indexed in the display above his bunk: four hundred and twelve separate data structures, all carrying his mother's archival certification from her Cicillian service years — before the [[Church of Purity]] had moved into the [[Helix Habitat]] and the maintenance of historical records had become a controlled function. The certification dates ran from six Vankh-Setts ago back to the beginning of her career. She had been building this for a long time.

He started with the population figures, because those he understood. A coupling specialist spent enough time cross-referencing technical documentation to know what honest data looked like: small inconsistencies accumulating in the direction of reality, numbers that wandered and corrected and wandered again the way actual systems do. What he found in the Helix Habitat demographic records was the opposite of this. Birth registers, mortality tallies, migration logs — the figures maintained a stability that no natural system could sustain. Wars reported in the historical narratives that produced zero net population change. A plague in the asteroid mining sectors, forty-two Vankh-Setts ago, with a recorded mortality of several thousand that the population registers simply did not reflect. Not corrected-for. Not compensated by migration. Not present.

Before and after. Identical.

He ran the figures again. Then he pulled the cross-reference data his mother had assembled alongside them — matching Cicillian internal projections against [[Purist]] population self-reporting, Church census against the independent academic estimates from institutions with no affiliation to either faction. Three incompatible methodologies, three mutually hostile data sources, all pointing at the same set of numbers.

Exactly identical.

He sat for a while with that.

He was a man who spent his professional life distinguishing between a drive system that was malfunctioning and a drive system that was behaving according to a design he had not yet located. The difference was everything. A malfunction was random and corrected by force. Design behaved according to rules that held even where you hadn't looked yet.

The demographic data was not malfunctioning. It was consistent. It was consistent in a way that required explanation, and the explanation was not random.

The door signal from [[Lin]].

He closed the crystal display and had not decided whether to hide the crystal by the time she entered, so he left it visible. She registered it with the mathematical precision that her enhancement grid performed automatically — cataloguing, cross-referencing, filing — and produced no outward response.

"Technician P-R-0-4-7-9. Administrative oversight visit, as scheduled." She settled into the chair across from his bunk with the particular spatial efficiency that characterized everything she did. Her enhancement patterns ran in tight sequences under her skin, the calculation matrices brightening as they processed the room — his tools, his display log, the crystal on the bunk beside him. "You appear to be conducting personal research."

"Archival work. My mother's historical records." He held to the same even register he used when working with a coupling whose specifications he hadn't fully committed to yet. "She was a Cicillian data specialist. I'm reviewing her documentation from before retirement."

"Academic curiosity." The phrase carried no warmth and precise ambiguity. Her patterns ran through something and settled. "Your professional assessments have continued to meet standard. Sector 7 proximity maintenance is your next assigned deployment. Relay Station One, original installation." A pause that contained neither hesitation nor reluctance — just the gap between two data points. "Consider this a career milestone. The oldest [[Vankh proximity]] site still in operational use. Very few specialists receive assignment there without seniority prerequisite."

"Why me?"

Her arrays cycled. "Your harmonic sensitivity ratings are exceptional. The Station's original coupling architecture requires non-standard diagnostic capability." Which was true as a reason and explained nothing about the timing. She produced the assignment documentation and waited for him to confirm receipt. He did.

"The [[data crystal]]," she said, not quite in the same register as the rest of the conversation. It was the kind of observation that was not quite an observation. "Your mother's archive. Historical records of what period?"

"Pre-Expansion demographic data. Standard archival material."

"Mm." Her patterns ran through a calculation that resolved without any visible output. "If you require assistance processing archival data through official channels, administrative support is available." She rose, the meeting being concluded to whatever specification she held it against. "Report to Docking Bay Nine, Relay Station departure, one full cycle from now."

The door closed behind her with the exact absence of sound of someone who had practiced not announcing themselves.

[[Pro]] sat in the quiet.

He pulled the crystal display back up and found the figure that had stopped him before he heard the door signal: the disaster record from forty-two Vankh-Setts ago. The plague. The mortality figures. Before and after, identical.

He looked at it for a long time.

The plague was documented in the [[Purist]] archives as divine judgment, God's instrument of purity working through the population to remove the corrupted before the Prophet's vision could fully manifest. The Cicillian archives described it as a contained environmental event, handled efficiently by corporate health protocols, with mortality figures consistent with the standard actuarial model for that category of incident.

Two incompatible interpretations. Two hostile institutions. One set of numbers, and the numbers said: neither account was true.
