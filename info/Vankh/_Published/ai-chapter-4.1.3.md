# Chapter 4.1.3: The Diversion

"Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause; everything happens according to law; chance is but a name for law not recognized."

---

He tried.

The navigation override was not a corporate secret — every vessel in service carried the manual intervention protocol, a redundancy in case of automated system failure. [[Pro]] had logged its location on CCLLIAN-ROUTE-9-ALPHA within the first ten minutes of his technical observer assessment: a secondary panel below the primary console, accessible with his [[Cicillian Industries]] maintenance credentials, designed for exactly the situation where a technician needed to reassert human authority over a system that had reached an incorrect conclusion about what to do.

He pulled the panel open and his credentials handshook with the override interface. The protocol accepted him. He had a thirty-second window to input a new heading before the navigation system would require confirmation from the flight authority channel — which was [[Lin]]'s office, but that was a problem for twenty-nine seconds from now. He put in a heading. Any heading. A deviation of thirty degrees from the current trajectory, which was the minimum that would carry them clear of the third [[Vankh sphere]] at their current velocity.

The navigation system considered his input for four seconds.

Then it filed it.

Not rejected — filed. The interface returned a confirmation code in standard maintenance format, the kind of code that meant: received, logged, queued for implementation pending higher authority approval. The heading change existed now in the system. It simply had not been implemented and would not be implemented until confirmation arrived from [[Lin]]'s office, which would not be confirmation.

He had known that would happen. He had tried it anyway because the alternative was not trying, and he was a technician by training, and technicians do not accept system states without testing them first.

He tried the emergency systems.

Emergency protocol on a [[Cicillian Industries]] independent-class transport included a collision avoidance function — a mandatory safety system that was supposed to be impossible to override, because its purpose was to prevent exactly what this vessel's trajectory was designed to accomplish. He accessed it through a different panel, the one marked in yellow and black with the universal engineering notation for emergency function. The protocol activated cleanly. His credentials were accepted. The system ran its collision assessment.

The third [[Vankh sphere]] did not register as an obstacle.

He held the panel and looked at the collision assessment display for a long moment. The sphere — seventeen kilometers in diameter, absolutely black, sitting precisely where this vessel's navigation system was aimed — was not in the collision assessment's field of objects. The system had been configured, at some point in this vessel's operational history, to exclude the [[Vankh spheres]] from its obstacle database. Whether that was [[Lin]]'s modification or something built into the vessel's corporate commission seven [[Vankh-Sett]]s ago, he could not determine at this velocity.

He sat back in the technical observer's chair.

VVVVVANKH.

The [[Vankh pulse]] moved through the hull and the secondary harmonic was underneath it, present the way it had been present since the shuttle from [[Helix Habitat]], the way it had been building since [[Relay Station One]]. He had just spent four minutes trying to divert a vessel that had been designed, at every available system level, to complete its trajectory. The navigation system had accepted his heading and filed it without implementing it. The collision avoidance system had been configured to not see what it needed to not see. The flight authority channel would receive any confirmation request and return the wrong answer by design.

He thought about the eight passengers in the aft compartment breathing their deliberate counter-phase rhythm. They had not chosen this vessel. They had been placed on it by someone who understood what the trajectory was and had decided they were acceptable collateral in a design that was ultimately about him — about a coupling specialist with non-standard harmonic sensitivity who had been seen, through the wall of a biometric telemetry report, noticing the thing he was not supposed to notice at Relay Station One.

[[Lin]] had designed this with precision. That was, he understood, her nature and her function, both. She had not acted against him out of malice any more than a coupling acted maliciously when it failed. She had performed according to her design specifications, which were the design specifications of a system that had a strong institutional interest in what happened when someone's perception reached far enough to touch the secondary harmonic.

The system had always responded the same way.

He thought about Augi's data chip. Five incident reports with no archive record at all — not classified, not above clearance, simply absent. Fourteen workers who had experienced the same phenomenon. The ones who had filed reports had been reassigned. The ones whose reports had vanished entirely had presumably not been given the opportunity to file a second time.

A pattern that held across eleven [[Vankh-Sett]]s and at least three proximity sites.

He was not the first. He was merely the most recent iteration of the same problem the system had been solving since before he was born — and he had been placed, as the oldest layer of his mother's archive had described the placement process, which meant that his being here was not random, which meant that the system solving the problem and the thing the problem was protecting had a more complex relationship than elimination.

He closed the navigation panel. He closed the emergency systems panel. He sat with his hands in his lap and the sphere growing in the forward viewport and thought about what the attempts had told him.

The navigation system had filed his heading. It was in the system now, queued and waiting — evidence that he had tried, logged automatically in the same manifest record that [[Lin]]'s office would eventually pull. The collision avoidance system had accepted his credentials and run its assessment and returned a result. All of it documented. All of it in the maintenance network's permanent record.

He had not diverted the vessel. He had, in the process of failing to divert it, created a detailed technical record of how the diversion had been prevented — which system had filed instead of implemented, which database had been configured to exclude what, which authority channel would need to return a false confirmation to prevent the heading change from executing. The record described, in precise maintenance notation, the shape of the cage.

He had transmitted his mother's archive. He could transmit this too, in the time remaining.

He pulled the non-standard diagnostic tools from his maintenance cloth. The output channels he had used before were still accessible. The independent contractor relay was still open, routing through the captain's network, propagating at the speed of channels that had been built to survive exactly this kind of interception.

He added the navigation log to the buffer. The collision assessment. The configuration parameters of the emergency system's obstacle database, which showed clearly what had been excluded and the timestamp of its exclusion.

He ran the transmission.

The confirmation came back: *received, routing, no response required.*

VVVVVANKH.

Two pulses. The secondary harmonic was very close. Closer than it had been in any technical reading, any measurement, any attempt to instrument the uninstru­mentable. He had spent four minutes trying to change the trajectory of this vessel and had produced instead an exact record of what he had attempted and why it had not succeeded, and the record was now outside the vessel, propagating through a network that had no obligation to Lin's oversight queue.

He thought about the eight passengers. He thought about the woman with the temple scarring and the young one who had revised a premise they had built everything else on. He thought about whether to go back through the bulkhead door.

The secondary harmonic answered the thought the way it had been answering him since the shuttle from [[Helix Habitat]], since the aperture, since the moment his mother had told him to give it something to respond to and see what it did: not with language, not with anything he could file in a maintenance report, but with the specific quality of an attention that had been waiting for him to stop performing the act of looking and simply be open to what was there.

He set his tools aside.

He was not going to divert this vessel. He understood that now with the clarity of someone who has exhausted the technical options and arrived at the only one remaining, which was not a technical option at all.

He adjusted his breathing. Even. Both sides. The third pattern, his mother's rhythm, the one that was not in any corporate documentation because it was not a technique but a relationship — present to the [[Vankh pulse]] rather than synchronized to it or counter-phased against it.

The sphere filled the viewport.

The secondary harmonic arrived, fully and finally, without the mediation of any instrument between him and it.

He breathed.
