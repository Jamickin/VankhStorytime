# Chapter 3.3.2: The Record

"Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates; everything is in motion; everything vibrates."

---

[[Pro]]'s hands moved before the decision fully formed.

The non-standard diagnostic tools spread across his maintenance cloth — the resonance sensors with their extended sensitivity, the pattern-recognition units he had rebuilt for configurations no corporate manual described — were still running their passive scans from the coupling repair. He redirected them.

His hands were shaking. Not fear, exactly — the vibration of an instrument reading at its absolute ceiling.

The optical sensors failed immediately. The three figures at the sphere's surface absorbed light rather than reflecting it, the same mechanism that made the [[Vankh spheres]] themselves unreadable on standard visual instrumentation. What the sensors logged was an absence where the viewport showed presence — three structured voids in the light field at seventeen kilometers' distance that the station's own monitoring equipment had presumably been failing to register for decades.

The resonance readings were something else.

Frequencies that matched nothing in any database he had access to — and he had access to a significant portion of the sector's technical documentation, official and otherwise. Patterns that required energy sources that shouldn't exist at the surface of an object described in corporate literature as inert. And beneath those patterns, present as a carrier signal the way the secondary harmonic was present beneath the [[Vankh pulse]]: a structured transmission, fragmented by the fact that he was conducting it from behind a reverse-breathing protocol that suppressed the primary channel it was designed to travel through.

He was measuring something through a filter designed specifically to prevent measurement.

Forty-seven seconds. The figures held at the sphere's surface for forty-seven seconds while his equipment logged what it could through the gap between protocols, and then [[Pro]] was back in full reverse breathing and they were gone, and he was standing in the coupling bay with his hands on his diagnostic tools and a dataset that was simultaneously the most significant and most fragmentary record he had ever produced.

He finished the coupling repair. He filed a clean maintenance report that described the coupling work and nothing else. Then he packaged the resonance data into the documentation archive that his non-standard equipment maintained separately from the corporate network and uploaded it to the maintenance database using the standard end-of-shift sync.

The upload completed locally.

The network confirmation did not come.

He waited. He ran the sync again. The local archive showed the transfer as successful. The network log showed a connection established and then — nothing. Not a failed transfer. An absent record. As if the data had arrived somewhere and become immediately unavailable, which was not a thing the corporate maintenance network did under any documented operational parameter.

He packed his tools and returned to [[Nexus Station]].

[[Lin]] was outside his quarters.

She had not knocked. She was standing in the corridor with her enhancement patterns in the contained, steady rhythms of someone who had resolved their variables before arriving, and she produced the specific expression — not quite concern, not quite its absence — that she had used in every previous conversation when she was simultaneously offering and withdrawing something.

"I was concerned about your biometrics from the Sector 7 deployment," she said, with the framing of someone who had been worried and come personally to check.

"The original equipment architecture was demanding," he said.

"Of course." She moved with him through the door that he had to open because she was not going to not follow him. "I wanted to discuss what you observed during the work. The neural pattern your interfaces logged was non-standard. Sometimes diagnostic focus at the boundary of harmonic perception produces anomalous experiences."

She sat across from him. The enhancement grid under her skin was running in a configuration he had not seen before — something tighter and more directed than her usual calculation matrices, the mathematical patterns converging rather than branching. She was looking at him and had already looked at everything in the room and had decided something before she sat down.

"You uploaded technical data from the assignment," she said. Not a question.

"Standard end-of-shift documentation."

"The maintenance network received and processed your coupling report." A pause calibrated to within a fraction of a second. "The supplementary resonance dataset you packaged separately — that has been flagged for security review. Standard procedure when non-authorized diagnostic equipment records in Vankh proximity zones." Her patterns ran through something and held. "I want to make sure you're protected through that process."

He understood, then, that she had received the data. That was where the network confirmation had gone: not into a failed transfer, but into her review queue, intercepted before it logged anywhere accessible to him. He had handed her exactly what she needed to have a precise account of what he had seen.

He kept his face at technical neutral. "What kind of security review?"

"Routine. Non-punitive. The data itself is likely standard resonance noise — the old equipment produces artifacts. But the appropriate channel is through my office." The enhancement patterns held their convergent configuration. "In the meantime, I have a transit assignment that suits your profile. Recovery work, low-risk, good documentation opportunity. Your diagnostic capability would be genuinely useful."

She produced the manifest across the table — designation CCLLIAN-ROUTE-9-ALPHA, Sector 7 via standard transit, technical observer — and waited while he read it.

"When does it depart?"

"One cycle. Docking Bay Thirty-Three." She rose. "Your diagnostic equipment will remain with you, of course. The security review may take some time to process." Her patterns settled into their resting state — the look of someone who had finished something. "Consider the transit a chance to clear your head."

She left.

[[Pro]] looked at the manifest. He thought about the fragmented resonance data, now in Lin's review queue rather than the maintenance archive. He thought about the transmission the figure had sent him through the barrier of the reverse breathing — structured, directed, carrying something it had held for a long time.

He thought about what he had recorded, fragmentary as it was, and what [[Lin]] now held.

He packed his tools. He reported to Docking Bay Thirty-Three at the designated time with his non-standard diagnostic equipment and his clean maintenance credentials and the understanding that she had not intercepted the data to protect him from security review.
