# Chapter 3.3.1: The Suppression

"Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates; everything is in motion; everything vibrates."

---

[[Pro]] resolved the error sequence, logged the coupling repair as complete, and filed a clean maintenance report.

He wrote it the way a technician writes a report when the work went according to specification: coupling interfaces within tolerance, harmonic resonance corrected to standard, no anomalous readings during operation. He documented the fault sequence he had encountered and its resolution. He noted the non-standard equipment architecture and recommended that junior technicians be briefed on original harnessing system methodology before being assigned to Relay Station One.

He did not note the breathing deviation. He did not note the thirty-second window of anomalous biometric data that the station's telemetry had already recorded automatically, uploaded automatically, and incorporated into the maintenance network automatically before he had finished writing the second paragraph.

He knew about the biometric upload when it happened. Every certified Vankh-proximity technician was informed of the automatic telemetry system during their proximity certification, framed as a safety feature — the corporation monitoring its workers' physiological state during high-risk assignments, detecting Dilation Hysteria before the worker could deny it. He had always regarded it as an intrusion and a sensible precaution simultaneously, the way most corporate systems managed to be both things at once.

He had not, until now, thought carefully about what it recorded.

The upload confirmed three things that his report did not mention: that his breathing pattern had deviated from the approved proximity protocol for thirty-two seconds; that his heart rate had elevated to a level consistent with acute psychological distress; and that his neural activity during those thirty-two seconds showed a pattern his interfaces had logged as "unclassified resonance event — refer to specialist review."

He returned to his quarters on [[Nexus Station]] and sat with this.

The transmission from the figure at the sphere's surface — the one that had been something like communication and something like the secondary harmonic and something like neither — was not stored anywhere except in the particular arrangement of his own understanding. The station's equipment had not captured it. His diagnostic tools, untouched on the maintenance cloth, had not captured it. There was no record of what had actually happened in that thirty-two-second window, only the physiological evidence that something had.

Which was exactly what the biometric system had been designed to record.

[[Lin]]'s appointment request arrived eleven hours later, framed as a "post-deployment administrative follow-up" with a scheduling priority that was technically optional and practically not.

She was already seated in the consultation room when he arrived. Her enhancement patterns were running in the contained, even sequences he had come to associate with a decision already made — not the calculating brightness of active analysis but the steady rhythms of someone who had resolved the variables and was now moving into implementation. She had his telemetry displayed on the room's primary screen, though she did not look at it while he settled into the chair across from her.

"Your Sector 7 deployment registered a biometric anomaly," she said. Not a question.

"The original harnessing equipment presented a fault sequence outside standard documentation. The diagnostic process was cognitively demanding." He held the technical register the way he held a coupling during calibration — steady pressure, no sudden movements. "The neural activity flag is a known artifact of extended harmonic tracing on pre-standard architectures."

Her arrays ran through that. "Your respiratory deviation is not consistent with diagnostic cognitive load. The pattern suggests proximity response." The enhancement grid under her skin cycled toward something and steadied. "Have you experienced symptoms consistent with inappropriate biometric response during Vankh proximity work before?"

"No," he said, which was true.

She looked at him with the flat assessment of someone reading a readout that had already told her what she needed to know. "We take respiratory deviations in proximity contexts seriously. Dilation Hysteria risk is measurable and preventable with appropriate oversight." A pause. "I'm initiating a standard wellness protocol. Low-risk assignment, routine documentation. Nothing that will affect your standing."

She produced the transport manifest across the table. Designation CCLLIAN-ROUTE-9-ALPHA. Sector 7, via standard transit. Technical observer role. "Consider it recognition for the Relay Station work. Your coupling assessment there will inform the next maintenance cycle planning."

He looked at the manifest. "What's the cargo?"

"Corporate transit evaluation. Non-specialist passengers. FTL documentation requirements." Her patterns did not change. "You'll be the senior technical presence aboard."

He confirmed receipt of the assignment documentation. She rose with the particular efficiency of an appointment concluded.

At the door she paused — not quite the hesitation of a person who had forgotten something, more the deliberate rhythm of one who had planned where to put the last piece. "The anomalous neural event in your biometrics," she said, without turning. "If you experienced any unusual perceptual phenomena during the deviation window — visual, auditory, proprioceptive — the medical documentation should reflect that. For your own protection."

She left without waiting for a response.

[[Pro]] sat in the consultation room and understood that she already knew what he had experienced. And that she had not asked whether he had experienced it. She had told him what to do with it.

He thought about the figure at the sphere's surface. The quality of recognition it had transmitted: old, patient, something carried a long way in time. The transmission had included a quality he had not quite had words for until this moment, sitting in [[Lin]]'s consultation room after the conversation she had just conducted with perfect precision.

It had been a warning.

He filed no medical documentation. He reported to Docking Bay Thirty-Three at the designated time and did not tell anyone where he was going.
