# Chapter 4.1.1: The Aspirants

"Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause; everything happens according to law; chance is but a name for law not recognized."

---

The bulkhead door between the forward technical section and the aft compartment was not designed to contain passengers. It was designed to keep equipment vibrations from disturbing people trying to sleep on long transits. [[Pro]] pushed it open.

The eight passengers registered his entry with the particular stillness of people who have spent a long time in expectation of something, and have arrived at a condition beyond either calm or fear. The plain clothing, the absent augmentations, the deliberate out-of-phase breathing — closer now, he could hear the rhythm they were all keeping together, a counter-rhythm to the [[Vankh pulse]] that he recognized from the Church's purification protocols. It was not the Vankh proximity counter-rhythm, which suppressed perception; it was the Church's devotional breath, which was supposed to purify.

No one spoke. They watched him the way people watch an unexpected variable.

The oldest of them sat in the rearmost seat, a woman past middle age with the specific texture of skin around the temples that meant augmentation scarring — the rougher, self-closing wound of removed implants rather than the clean integrated seam of active ones. Her eyes were the only active thing about her; they moved to his technical badge, to his maintenance cloth, to the non-standard diagnostic equipment visible in his kit, and arrived at a careful, precise attention.

"You're not corporate transport evaluation," she said.

"Neither are you," [[Pro]] said.

He pulled the fold-down seat beside the door and sat in it. The youngest person in the compartment — barely past Academy age, with the unfinished quality of someone still waiting to understand who they were — watched this with an expression that couldn't decide between wariness and relief.

[[Pro]] set his diagnostic equipment on his knees and opened the [[data crystal]] display on the integrated reader, rotating it so they could see. The demographic records — his mother's archive, the four hundred and twelve data structures assembled across a career spent looking carefully at numbers that did not behave.

"What is that?" the young one asked.

"Historical records. Population data." [[Pro]] scrolled to the page he had stopped on, in his quarters, after [[Lin]] had left. "You can pick any year in this archive and check the population figure against the year before it and the year after it. The number will be the same." He found the disaster record. "This is forty-two Vankh-Setts ago. A documented plague in the asteroid mining sectors. The Church's archive describes it as divine judgment — thousands dead, the corrupted removed before the Prophet's vision could fully manifest. Cicillian's archive describes it as a contained environmental event, handled efficiently by corporate health protocols." He let them look at the before-and-after census figures. "Neither account mentions that the population count did not change."

Silence.

"Not approximately the same," he said. "Identical."

The older woman was not looking at the display. She was looking at [[Pro]] with the focused attention of someone who has been looking at this problem from a different direction for a long time and has just identified where the two lines of approach converge.

"There are no birth records in that archive," she said.

[[Pro]] looked at her.

"I served in Cicillian data processing for twenty-two Vankh-Setts," she said. "Before." The word carried the weight of the thing that had been taken from her. "Demographic records, population management, census reconciliation. I processed millions of entries. Birth records, intake registrations, individual identification assignments." She paused. "I never processed a birth record. I understood this as a gap in the documentation system, a clerical division I didn't have access to. It wasn't until after — when they took the implants — that I understood there was no gap." She looked at the census figures on the display and then looked past them. "We don't come from births. We don't reproduce. We are placed."

[[Pro]] had not thought about this. He had thought about populations that did not change, about disaster records that the numbers refused to acknowledge, about history that followed geometric progressions too precise for chaos. He had not thought about where any of them had come from.

He was trying to find the place in his training where reproduction had been discussed, and it wasn't there. It had never been there. A category so absent from his education that its absence had never presented itself as a shape. The crystal's archive had population intake records, designation assignments, Academy enrollment figures — and no births anywhere in four hundred and twelve data structures spanning generations.

"We were made," he said.

"We were made," the older woman agreed. Not with the Prophet's register of heresy and divine horror. With something quieter than that. With the tone of a data specialist who has found an answer she suspected a long time ago.

The young one in the corner said nothing. Their expression had resolved — not wariness, not relief. Something harder and less comfortable, the look of someone revising a premise they had built everything else on.

"We were made for what?" someone near the middle of the compartment asked. A man with the careful build of someone who had done physical labor for decades, whose augmentation scarring ran along both forearms. His voice carried no challenge — just the honest question.

[[Pro]] looked at the forward viewport through the open bulkhead door. The third [[Vankh spheres|Vankh sphere]] was not yet visible — they were still in transit — but the secondary harmonic was present in his perception the way it had been since the shuttle from [[Helix Habitat]], the way everything he had measured and not measured had been pointing toward.

"I think," he said carefully, "we were made to be here. Specifically here. In this moment." He looked back at the compartment. "Not all of us. I think I am. I think you are for different reasons that serve the same end. But I don't think the trajectory of this vessel is what you believe it is."

One pulse. The VVVVVANKH moved through the hull, and in its wake the secondary harmonic was very close.

"Then what is it?" the older woman asked.

[[Pro]] thought about the figures at the sphere's surface at [[Relay Station One]]. Old and patient. What they had transmitted: not a warning. Proof. What the sphere's surface was covered in: every consciousness that had reached this moment without the right configuration. Every attempt at the threshold that had arrived with the wrong orientation — terror, or devotion, or the need to be right — and had not completed the integration. They were not haunting the sphere. They were at the threshold, which was where the threshold is, which meant they had made it as far as the threshold.

Just not through it.

"I think it's what the whole system was built for," he said. "I think they wanted someone to arrive here without fear and without doctrine. Neither Cicillian nor Church." He looked at the woman with the temple scarring. "Someone who could hear it."

She was quiet for a moment. Then: "Can you?"

The secondary harmonic arrived — fully present, as it had been in the forward section, as it had been building toward since Relay Station One — and [[Pro]] realized he was already in the third breathing pattern. Even. Both sides. In phase with the Pulse rather than synchronized to it or counter-phased against it.

"Yes," he said.

VVVVVANKH.

The sphere became visible in the forward viewport as the transit completed — perfect, absolute, dominant. The woman with the temple scarring looked at it with the expression of someone who has found the right word for something they have been failing to name for a very long time.

"Then go back," she said. "Do what you came to do."
