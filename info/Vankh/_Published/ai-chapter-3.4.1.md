# Chapter 3.4.1: The Captain's Route

"Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause; everything happens according to law; chance is but a name for law not recognised."

---

The address was buried in the independent relay network at three levels of misdirection that [[Pro]] had memorized rather than recorded, because the kind of address that didn't log its own activity was also the kind that didn't survive being written down. He had used it once before, to acknowledge receipt of transit intelligence that had arrived through the captain's contacts without a sender designation. He had not expected to use it again so soon.

He composed the message the way he composed maintenance logs: no excess, no hedging, all the information that needed to be transmitted and nothing that didn't. The assessment cycle moved up, extended to full coverage, his parents on the list. The window before their residential sector reached the evaluation teams. What he needed was a route past [[Helix Habitat]] that did not touch the corporate manifest system at any point, from departure to arrival to documentation.

The response came in under two hours.

The captain was three sectors out on a supply haul to the independent settlements in the outer ring — running the kind of circuit that corporate transit records acknowledged existed without being specific about the routes, because corporate transit records were maintained by the same system that had built the manifest requirements that independent operators systematically circumvented. He knew people. That was the totality of what he offered and it was, [[Pro]] understood, the only thing that mattered.

What followed was a logistics conversation conducted entirely in the language of physical systems, because that was the only language either of them trusted fully. The captain described the independent transit network that operated outside corporate manifests in the same register he used to describe drive maintenance — as a set of relationships with specific load tolerances and specific failure modes, navigated by people who understood what each connection could carry and never asked it to carry more. He named the nodes without naming them: a freight coordinator he trusted, a route that appeared in the supply chain documentation as a mining-support loop and existed in practice as a channel for people who needed to move without being seen. The timing. The documentation: maintenance-supply authorization, the same certification class [[Pro]] had been building for the medicine shipments, a cover that held because it had always held.

He asked only one question. "Will they trust a stranger's ship?"

[[Pro]] thought about his father at the base of the ventilation ladder, the mineral-cold air, the instruction given with the particular economy of a man who had spent years working in channels where what you said could be recorded but your tone could not. He thought about his mother in the back room of their quarters, her enhancement arrays dim, her voice clear, describing how to hold the kind of attention that the secondary harmonic required. Two people who had spent years building the infrastructure that would get them out of [[Helix Habitat]] if they needed to leave it — and had been doing so before he understood that the infrastructure existed.

"My father will," he said.

He did not say: I don't know how clear she is right now, whether the lucidity interval has held, whether she would recognize the captain's vessel as safe passage or as one more unfamiliar thing in a period when everything unfamiliar registered as threat. He did not say it because he could not say it and remain operational, and because his father would know how to handle it, and because some variables in a system you cannot fully observe have to be held as known when the alternative is doing nothing.

The window was two [[Vankh-Sett]]s out — the edge of the assessment cycle's forward movement into the residential sectors, the last viable gap before the Church evaluation teams would be in his parents' section of [[Helix Habitat]]. The captain ran the logistics against his existing route and confirmed the transit was possible. The documentation structure would take one Vankh-Sett to build through the freight coordinator's booking system. They confirmed the contact structure, the status signals, the withdrawal sequence if anything changed and the extraction needed to be aborted before it completed.

[[Pro]] held the plan in his mind the way he held a coupling configuration before committing it to the maintenance record — checking each element against the next, tracing the dependencies, identifying the single points where a failure would cascade and the redundancies built around them. It held. It was not elegant and it was not safe in any absolute sense, but no extraction from a habitat under Church assessment was safe in any absolute sense, and what it was instead was executable.

He waited.

At the first interval of the second [[Vankh-Sett]], the captain's relay carried a message in a format [[Pro]] had not seen before and recognized immediately: his father's hand code. The particular pattern of line breaks and spacing that the two of them had developed over years of communication through channels that were not designed for privacy — a system of small variations that looked, to any automated parser, like idiosyncratic punctuation.

_Postponement. They know the route. Don't try again until I signal._

[[Pro]] read it twice and then put the display down and did not reach for it again.

The route was blown, or was close enough to blown that his father had decided the difference wasn't worth the risk. Someone had seen something — a document query, a booking pattern, a face in the wrong corridor at the wrong time. The assessment cycle postponement that followed might be real or might be a Church administrative decision or might be exactly what it appeared to be: the particular kind of apparent reprieve that made a person stand down while whoever had seen the route processed what they had found. He could not tell which from here. He could not tell which from there, which was why his father had made the decision himself and transmitted it in a format that told [[Pro]] the channel itself was now suspect.

He cancelled the freight coordinator's booking through the standard logistics interface. Supply route adjustment, standard withdrawal. Nothing that looked like anything except a routine change in a routine parts shipment.

The captain's network was intact. The route had been pulled back from, not exposed and burned — there was a distinction there, specific and important, the difference between a channel that was compromised and a channel that had been deliberately not used before it could be compromised. His father had made that distinction himself. Had decided, with whatever information he had available to him at that moment in [[Helix Habitat]], that the route was more valuable intact and unused than consumed in a single extraction, and had transmitted the decision in a format that required [[Pro]] to understand why before he could understand what it was asking him to do.

He sat with that.

The medicine route was still functional — he would not know for certain until the next delivery cycle, but the captain's network and the medicine shipment channel were layered differently, serving different functions through different operational structures. His father's instruction had been specific to the extraction route. The medicine shipments would continue until they couldn't.

He thought about dozens of people who were not his parents. The pre-standard couplings in the ventilation infrastructure below [[Helix Habitat]]'s residential levels. His father managing, from inside a habitat under Church assessment, the flow of people through a network that [[Pro]] had not known existed until his father had shown him the base of the ladder and the cold air and the specific weight of a responsibility held by one person alone for a very long time.

His father had not come out in order to keep the route available for the others. Had made that decision without consulting [[Pro]], which was its own kind of statement — a statement that said: I trust you to understand this when I tell you, and I am not asking for your agreement because I have already decided. The decision had been made at the bottom of the ladder, probably, or earlier, or at the moment the assessment cycle moved up and expanded and someone in the network calculated what extraction would cost the route and what the route was worth to the people still moving through it.

It was, [[Pro]] thought, the most his father had ever said to him.

He held the dark relay interface and the withdrawn booking and the captain's intact network and his father's hand code and the two [[Vankh-Sett]] window that had now closed, and understood that what had happened was not a failed extraction. It was an extraction that had been considered and assessed by someone with more information than he had, and deliberately not completed.

He did not know if that was better. He knew it was true.

He opened the assignment documentation [[Lin]] had sent him. Docking Bay Thirty-Three. The manifest. The route. He read it the way he read a coupling he had not yet committed to — not looking for what he expected to find, but present to what was actually there. And what was actually there, when he read it that way, described a trajectory that his father had told him something about at the base of a ladder, in mineral-cold air, before either of them had known what it meant.
