# Chapter 3.1.3: The Interval

"Everything is dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites; like and unlike are the same."

---

His father left for the maintenance level before the second [[Vankh-Sett]].

He said it the way he said most necessary things — economically, without the weight that the statement probably deserved. "Stay with her until she wakes. She'll have a clear window." He had already checked the corridor in both directions before he pulled on the worn jacket he wore in the maintenance passages, its pockets holding the small tools that had nothing to do with the work he told people he was doing down there. Then he was gone, and [[Pro]] was alone in the small room with the photographs on the wall and the [[breathing pattern]] chart beside the door and the faint bioluminescent pulse of his mother's enhancement arrays in the back room.

He sat beside the bed and did what he had always done when he did not know what else to do: he paid attention.

Her name in the corporate system was C-A-R-4-5-8. In the medical records it was [[Pro's Mother]]. He had been calling her something else, something private, since before he had words for it, a name that had never needed to be a designation because it was only ever used by one person, in the particular register of someone addressing the most specific version of another human being. He did not think of the corporate name in this room. The corporate name was for the systems that needed to classify her. This room was not a system.

The enhancement arrays dimmed further as he watched — the resting cycle his father had described, the one the temporal stabilizers created, a controlled interval where the rejection process slowed enough for her mind to surface. He had learned to read her sleeping breath the way he read drive coupling output: the shallow rapid cycling meant the arrays were still processing, managing the rejection chemistry at the level where it met the neural tissue. The slower breath meant she was beneath that, in the interval.

She opened her eyes at the fourth [[Vankh]] pulse after his father left.

The clarity was immediate. Not the gradual surfacing of someone waking from sleep but the specific, pointed alertness that the temporal stabilizer created — a window, his father had said, and that was exactly what it was: something opening in a wall that was otherwise closed.

"You're still here," she said.

"Father's on the maintenance level."

She turned her head and looked at him with the eyes that had frightened him as a child because they seemed to see past whatever surface he was presenting, and which he understood now as a consequence of a lifetime spent reading archival data — the practice of looking past notation to what the notation recorded. "How long do I have?"

"He said you'd have a clear window. He didn't say how long."

She accepted this and settled into a position that meant she was going to use the time she had. "You've been hearing it."

It was not a question.

"Since the last transit."

"Describe how."

He thought about this carefully, the way he thought about a coupling reading he was trying to render accurately. "Not as sound. As — a quality of the space beneath the primary pulse. Like the difference between a coupling that is running and a coupling that is running correctly. The secondary phenomenon isn't added to the pulse. It exists in the relationship between the pulse and whatever the pulse is bouncing off of."

She was quiet for a moment, in the way she was quiet when something had been confirmed. "I hear it differently now," she said. "Since the arrays started failing. The filtration they applied — the standard calibration arrays that all the corporate enhancements run — it was filtering it below a threshold I never knew I was operating above." She looked at the ceiling, which was plain white plaster, the habitat's original surface material under every renovation. "When they began to reject, the first thing I noticed was the noise. What I thought was noise. The failing calibration letting in what it had always been trained to exclude." A pause. "It took me eighteen months to understand I wasn't losing resolution. I was gaining it. The noise had always been there. The enhancement arrays had been keeping it below the threshold of awareness because that was their function."

"Corporate issued the calibration standards."

"Corporate inherited them from whatever built the original design specifications. The filtering has been present in every enhancement iteration since the first documented [[Cicillian]] augmentation protocols." She said it without bitterness. As a data specialist would say it: here is what the record shows. "It isn't malice. I don't believe it's malice. I think the design specifications were written by people who understood what the enhancement arrays would encounter in proximity to the [[Vankh pulse]], and who decided that the correct response was to buffer it out." She turned her head toward him. "What they didn't account for was that the buffer could fail gracefully. That someone might come through the failure and find the thing they'd been buffering."

"Or that someone without the enhancements might hear it from the beginning," [[Pro]] said. He was thinking of the [[Purist]] adherents, their augmentation scars, the specific quality of what he had read in the confiscated testimonies at [[Nexus Station]].

"Yes." She had thought about this too. "The [[Church of Purity]] has an answer that is correct in its diagnosis and catastrophically wrong in its response. The enhancement arrays do interfere with perception of the secondary harmonic. The corrective action is not to remove the enhancement arrays. The corrective action is to learn to listen around them."

"Can you? While they're still functional?"

"I couldn't. I tried for years and couldn't. My calibration was too thorough." A small movement that might have been the residue of a smile, filtered through the constraint of neural rejection. "But you didn't start with corporate calibration at the same depth I did. And you've spent eleven years working in physical proximity to drive systems that reproduce the pulse at close range. Your enhancement profile is lighter, and your exposure to the harmonic environment is much longer." She looked at him with the precise attention of someone who had been working toward a conclusion for a long time. "You're closer to hearing it clearly than I ever was. Without going through what I went through to get here."

The [[Vankh pulse]] came through the walls of the family quarters with the particular quality it had here — heavier than on [[Nexus Station]], slower in its attenuation through the habitat's older hull material. [[Pro]] felt it in his chest the way he always did, and beneath it, present as it had been since the transit, the secondary harmonic.

He held the shape of his mother's question in his mind and did what she had told him to do on his last visit — not reaching for an instrument, just attending. Present to it. The way he had been present to it on the shuttle out, when the aperture collapsed and the harmonic had shifted in response to the quality of his attention rather than the output of his scanner.

"It changes when I attend to it without instruments," he said.

"Yes."

"Not in a way I can measure."

"No. You can't measure attention with the things you use to measure coupling harmonics." She said it gently, which was the register she used when she was correcting a category error rather than a factual one. "What changes?"

He tried to describe it accurately. "The quality becomes more specific. As if there's a difference between the signal and the signal-directed-at-me, and when I stop using instruments the second thing becomes available." He paused. "It's aware of the difference between the instrument and the mind behind the instrument."

"Yes," she said. "That is exactly it."

They sat with that for a moment.

"The crystal," he said. "You built the archive because you'd heard it through the failing arrays and wanted to understand what you were hearing."

"I built the archive because the archive was what I had to give you." She said it with the particular precision of someone who has considered this formulation for a long time. "I couldn't give you functioning enhancement arrays. I couldn't give you corporate access or proximity credentials or the eleven years of drive work that taught you to read harmonic signatures. Those were already yours. What I could give you was the record I had assembled, and the evidence that what you would hear was real, and the name of where to go to test it." She paused. "Relay Station One. Where the original harnessing equipment still runs. Where the channel between the primary pulse and whatever responds to it has not been rebuilt or recalibrated since the first proximity researchers decided to see what they could learn if they listened carefully enough."

"And stopped using the protocol that filtered it out."

"And stopped using the protocol," she agreed.

The window was running out. He could see it in the rhythm of her breathing — the enhancement arrays cycling back toward active suppression, the temporal stabilizer's interval narrowing. She had used it well. She had spent it the way she spent everything, efficiently and toward purpose, without sentimentality about the limitation.

"The data crystal," she said, before the clarity had fully receded. "You've read part of it."

"The population records. I haven't reached the older layers yet."

"Go deeper." Her voice was already carrying the slight detachment of someone beginning to surface from the window's edge back into the general weather of her condition. "There is a document near the base of the archive — pre-Consolidation notation, the oldest material I could recover — that uses a word I couldn't find in any current reference system. I left it untranslated. You'll know which one when you find it."

"How will I know?"

"Because you'll have heard the secondary harmonic clearly enough by then to recognize what the word is pointing at."

The enhancement arrays brightened toward their working state. The clarity faded not dramatically but with the specific quality of a window narrowing — not closing, exactly, but returning to its ordinary dimensions. She closed her eyes. The bioluminescent pulse slowed into its maintenance rhythm.

[[Pro]] sat beside her in the quiet room with the photographs on the wall, the breathing chart beside the door, the distant sound of the habitat's recycling infrastructure maintaining the atmosphere that allowed all of this to continue.

The secondary harmonic was present. Patient. It had been present, he understood now, in every room he had ever been in. He had simply been calibrated to exclude it.

He was beginning to learn what his mother had learned through loss: that exclusion was a design choice, not a law.
