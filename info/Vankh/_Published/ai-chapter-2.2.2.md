# Chapter 2.2.2: The Prophet's Archive

"As above, so below; as below, so above."

---

The confiscated stacks occupied a corridor between maintenance section G-7 and the atmospheric recycling plant — not officially a storage area, not officially anything, which was why no one had bothered to route proper surveillance through it. [[Pro]] used his technical credentials to pass the access panel and spent forty minutes with a palm-light and a dust-grey archive crate that some [[Nexus Station]] administrator had labeled, in red ink that had faded to rust, PROPHETIC MATERIALS — CONFISCATED PER ORDER 8819-ALPHA.

Inside: printed testimonies. Recording chips with handwritten indices on their cases. Maps of transit corridors annotated in a cramped script. Copies of engineering diagrams with marginal notes that were not engineering — they were, he realized, attempts to turn data into prayer.

He found the transit log from the incident twenty-three [[Vankh-Sett]]s before the [[Church of Purity]] had taken its current form, when its future leader had still held a maintenance worker designation and had been aboard a corporate vessel experiencing catastrophic [[drive coupling]] failure during [[FTL]] transit. The log was fragmentary and partially corrupted, but legible enough.

The readings were familiar.

He had to look at them twice to be certain. The secondary harmonic was there, stronger than anything he had recorded — because the failing [[drive coupling]] had stripped away every layer of buffering that normally kept it below the specification floor. Naked in the data. Undeniable. Patterned. The mathematical relationships were the same. The responsiveness to the scan was the same. Whatever had answered [[Pro]]'s instruments through the viewport of a station berth had answered through the fractured systems of a failing corporate transport twenty-three Vankh-Setts ago, when a maintenance worker who had been too frightened to observe it with anything but his unmediated nervous system had named it God.

[[Dilation Hysteria]] did that, [[Pro]] understood. It did not degrade perception — it stripped away the calibration that kept perception inside the approved range. The prophetic experience was not hallucination. It was unfiltered contact. He had suspected this since reading his own transit data. Seeing it confirmed in someone else's breakdown, from the other side of a theological divide, made it something beyond suspicion.

[[Pro]] sat on the floor of the maintenance corridor and read the original testimony that had founded the [[Church of Purity]].

The experience of the harmonic as described was accurate. The panic was accurate, the flooding of the visual cortex, the temporal displacement, the sense of being observed by something vast and organized — all of it matched what [[Pro]]'s own training had let him survive and his instruments had let him analyze. What the future [[The Prophet]] had made of it was something else entirely: a divine voice speaking through the corrupted flesh of technology, a warning that augmentation was an offense against the natural rhythm of creation, a calling to return humanity to its baseline state so that the true music could be heard again.

He had been partially right. The rhythm was real. The presence behind it was real. The sense that technology was intermediary between the human and something beyond — partially correct.

What he had concluded from those premises was understandable and completely wrong.

And from that misreading — one man's encounter with the same phenomenon [[Pro]] had measured, filtered through terror and inadequate instrumentation and a worldview that had no framework for what he was perceiving — a movement had grown that was now surgically removing implants from the unwilling in the name of purification.

[[Cicillian Industries]]'s founding had done the same thing in reverse, [[Pro]] thought, pushing himself upright and replacing the crate. The corporation had built its empire on the [[Vankh pulse]] — measuring, exploiting, commercializing the rhythm without understanding what generated it or why. Treating an engineered phenomenon as natural law because the alternative was too destabilizing to consider. The [[Church of Purity]] said: it is sacred, do not touch it. Cicillian said: it is a resource, extract from it. Two opposing institutions. Same phenomenon. Symmetrically opposite responses. Both wrong.

[[Correspondence]]: as above, so below. As the scale of the cosmos, so the scale of human institutions. The experiment had arranged itself, [[Pro]] reflected, very neatly. Two reactions to the same stimulus, carefully separated, watched from a sufficient distance to yield clean data. Neither faction ever quite asking what the stimulus actually was.

He secured the access panel behind him and walked back through the blue concourses with the particular quiet of someone who has just watched a large piece of the world fall into place.
