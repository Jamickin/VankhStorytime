# Chapter 3.1: The Helix

"Everything is dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites; like and unlike are the same."

---

The shuttle from [[Nexus Station]] to [[Helix Habitat]] ran a route so short that [[Pro]] barely had time to re-enter the breathing pattern before the transit aperture opened and closed. The [[Vankh pulse]] was the same as ever. The secondary harmonic was there beneath it, the quiet answer that had followed him out of instruments and into unaided perception. He was beginning to believe he would carry it for the rest of his life — that the frequency had always been present and he had simply learned, in the dark of his quarters with a scanner running past its ceiling, to hear it.

[[Helix Habitat]] approached through the forward viewport as a ring structure orbiting a dim stellar body, its outer hull carrying the scars of contested governance: Cicillian-spec polymer on the older sections, later additions faced with the rough stone that the [[Church of Purity]] had adopted as its material of ideology, and between them, bolted steel that was neither doctrine — just the practical language of people making do. He had grown up here. It had never looked so small.

The checkpoint was new.

Two officers in the grey-brown uniform of Church enforcement stood at the disembarkation threshold, running identity wands over each arriving passenger with the quiet, focused certainty of people doing God's work. [[Cicillian Industries]] citizens with visible augmentations were waved to a secondary line for additional processing. The [[Purist adherents]] moved through cleanly. [[Pro]] fell into the small middle group — a corporate technician with modifications minimal by augmentation standards, excessive by Purist ones — and submitted to the wand without expression.

"Enhancement inventory?" the officer asked, her eyes fixed on the readout rather than his face.

"Drive specialist modifications. Sensory calibration for [[FTL travel]], neural timing regulators, sub-dermal radiation shielding." He recited them without inflection. "All documented under Cicillian contract. Maintenance credential P-R-0-4-7-9."

She entered something, let him pass without a second look, and turned to the next traveler.

[[Pro's Father]] — K-H-U-9-9-8 — was not in the family quarters when [[Pro]] arrived. A neighbor met him at the door with the particular expression of someone who has learned to move carefully, to knock before speaking, to look twice at the corridor behind a visitor before saying anything at all. "He's on the maintenance level," she said. "Below the recycling plant. He said to wait."

[[Pro]] waited in the familiar small room — photographs on the wall that were physical, not projected, because his parents had never trusted projections — and looked at the things that had been there his whole life: his father's Academy reference manuals, his [[Pro's Mother]]'s archive chips in their labeled cases, the [[breathing pattern]] chart mounted beside the door that neither of his parents technically needed any longer and neither had ever taken down.

His father arrived forty minutes later, moving with the slight compensating shift in his gait that the failing Motion Enhancer required — a shift [[Pro]] had watched for so long that he only now noticed it by reminding himself to look. He was carrying a sealed container that he set inside a wall cavity [[Pro]] had not known existed before locking it with a code he did not enter until he had checked the corridor in both directions.

"What are you moving?" [[Pro]] asked.

"People, mostly." His father settled into the chair across from him and looked at his son with the appraising steadiness that had never been warm in any obvious way and had always been, underneath that, entirely steady. "Enhancement rejection that qualifies for purification under the new codes. We get them out before the assessment cycle."

"Out to where?"

"Independent networks. There are more of them than [[Cicillian Industries]] or the Church either one would like to believe." He leaned forward, elbows on his knees. "How is your mother?"

[[Pro]] set the maintenance container on the table between them — its Cicillian certification label reading as a standard parts shipment to any scanner that looked. "Temporal stabilizers. Licensed work, good compound. This should buy her several months if the rejection doesn't accelerate."

His father took the container and held it with both hands, looking at it for a long moment, and said nothing. It was the kind of silence [[Pro]] had learned to read as a very precise form of gratitude, from a man who had decided somewhere long ago that sentiment was too expensive to indulge when circumstances required all available resources elsewhere. He had always been this way — his father, who had built Pro's love of drive systems by bringing home technical manuals from the maintenance facility and spreading them on this same table.

His mother was asleep when [[Pro]] reached the back room. Her augmentations had dimmed to their resting state, the faint bioluminescence of memory enhancement arrays reduced to a slow, barely perceptible pulse — not the failure-mode flicker that had frightened him on his last visit, but something quieter, more controlled. The temporal stabilizers would reach her in the next treatment cycle.

He sat beside her and watched the pulse of the enhancement arrays, and thought about the transit log in the confiscated [[Purist]] stacks, and the scanner readings in his quarters, and the meteorite scarring on the captain's hull, and the way the secondary harmonic had changed when he focused his attention on it — responding not to the instrument, but to the mind behind the instrument.

She opened her eyes.

The clarity was immediate — not the partial-focus of someone surfacing from illness, but the specific, directed alertness that had frightened him as a child because it seemed to belong to someone who could see things he could not yet perceive. The same eyes that had pressed the [[data crystal]] into his hands with wild certainty he had written off as deterioration.

"You heard it," she said. It was not a question.

"Yes."

"The secondary note." Her voice was low and precise, her neural arrays still dim but her eyes entirely present. "I heard it first through the failing arrays. I thought it was degradation — random noise, artifacts. Then I realized it was consistent. Patterned." A pause that held no confusion in it. "It tracks attention, Pro. I tested it. Every time I focused directly on it, it changed. Every time I stopped attending, it returned to baseline."

"I know," he said. "I've measured it."

"You have an advantage I didn't." She turned her head and looked at him with those eyes that had seen something he was still only beginning to approach. "You have instruments. But try it without them. Give it something to respond to, and see what it does with the response."

He held her hand and said nothing for a while.

When he left, his father walked him to the checkpoint threshold and stood there with the contained deliberateness of someone who had learned that displays of feeling at boundaries tend to attract attention.

"Come back before the assessment cycle," his father said. "If the situation escalates—" He stopped.

"I'll come back," [[Pro]] said.

The shuttle out of [[Helix Habitat]] rode the shortest transit route in the sector, barely long enough to achieve [[FTL]] state before it dropped back into normal space. But the [[Vankh's Eye]] was there in the aperture all the same — that perfect dark at the center of the transition tunnel, the void that had always been described to him as an absence.

He did not open the scanner.

He held the shape of a question in his mind — not words, not analysis, but the quality of attention he brought to a coupling with an irregular harmonic: present to it, available, calibrated, not performing the act of looking but simply being open to what was there. He directed it at the Eye the way his father had taught him to hold a tool: lightly enough to feel what it told you.

The secondary harmonic shifted.

Not the pattern-change of a system responding to an instrument. Smaller. More precise. A quality of attention returned from the direction of the dark — brief, exact, unmistakable. Not an answer in any language he possessed. Something that acknowledged the question had been received, and that the question had been noticed by the right organ.

Not the scanner. The mind behind it.

The aperture collapsed. Normal space returned. The [[Helix Habitat]] fell away behind him in the dark.

[[Pro]] sat in the shuttle's forward row, his hands folded in his lap, and understood that his mother had not been the only one doing the testing.
