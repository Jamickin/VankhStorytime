# Chapter 1.2.1: The Station

"Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall; the pendulum swing manifests in everything."

---

[[Nexus Station]] did not welcome so much as absorb.

[[Pro]] cleared customs in thirty seconds — neural handshake, biometric match, enhancement inventory confirmed — and stepped into the terminal's current. The concourse moved around him like a living thing: [[Cicillian]] citizens sliding through coordinated patterns their augmentations made invisible to him, [[Purist]] travelers bunching in tight clusters near the physical signage, independent haulers angling for the freight side exits with their cargo manifests already glowing on handheld screens. He let the current carry him as far as the maintenance access panel near the east wall, then ducked through it.

The noise cut off like a blade.

Down here the station spoke a different language. Conduit ran in thick braids overhead — power, atmosphere, data, waste — and the floor was reinforced polymer gone matte with use. He had been in these corridors a dozen times over as many [[Vankh-Sett]]s, and he moved through them the way he moved through shuttle drive bays: with a technician's reflex toward whatever was wrong.

Something was wrong.

He noticed it before he could name it. A texture to the corridor walls that didn't match the battered-utility aesthetic he expected. He stopped at a junction and ran his fingers along the seam where two wall sections met. The join was tight. Not maintenance-tight — the serviceable metal-on-metal of a repair — but construction-tight, the invisible seam of original fabrication. He took out his scanner on reflex and held it to the joint.

The reading came back: [[Helix Habitat]] alloy. Pre-Consolidation grade. The same molecular signature the entire section beneath him shared, all the way back to the access panel he had entered through.

[[Pro]] moved more slowly now, scanner active, watching the numbers. Three junctions, two pressure doors, a long straight run of corridor that passed below the [[Cicillian]] concourse and emerged on the far side beneath the [[Purist]] enclave. The alloy signature did not change. The fabrication methodology did not change. The thermal expansion coefficients, the structural load tolerances, the gauge of the conduit mounts — all identical, all the way across a station whose surface announced three completely different civilizations.

The factional divide was a skin. The skeleton underneath was one thing.

He filed it the way he filed anomalous drive readings — carefully, without urgency, without letting the implication run ahead of the data. Whatever this meant could wait until he knew more. He had learned, in eight [[Vankh-Sett]]s of coupling work, that the universe did not rush to explain itself, and that technicians who assumed explanations before the data was complete made errors that killed people.

He made it to the [[Mechanic]]'s section in eleven minutes. Her entrance was behind a nutrition dispensary — a nutrition dispensary that had never, in his experience, served a customer while he was watching. The smell of recycled protein supplements provided cover for the subtler smells of solder and synthetic tissue beneath. He pressed the contact plate, gave his identifier, waited.

The door opened.

[[Mechanic]] [[Augi]] had been in the middle of something involving a neural array spread across a clean tray, each component separated with a precision that matched what her hands were now capable of. Her original face was not available for reading — the modifications she wore ran too deep and too far back for that. What remained communicated through posture and the angle of her hands, and right now they communicated that she had been expecting him.

"Technician," she said. "Your mother's prescription changed. The compound became harder to source." She moved to a locked drawer without being asked. "The price reflects that."

[[Pro]] set his payment chip on the clean section of her worktable. She acknowledged it without looking at it. He watched the mathematics of her hands — the fluid micro-adjustments she made around the neural array, touching components without disturbing them, feeling through augmented fingertips what baseline touch could never detect.

"The maintenance corridors in this section," he said. "Original construction."

"Yes." She continued working.

"Same design all the way through. Under the [[Cicillian]] side, under the [[Purist]] side. One engineer's hand."

"One hand, one mind, one moment," [[Augi]] said. "Whatever argument the factions are having up there, they are having it inside someone else's house." She paused, her fingers resting on the array. "They've never asked whose house it is. People generally don't."

She set a sealed pharmaceutical pack next to the payment chip. [[Pro]] did not reach for it yet.

"The oxygen recycling system," he said. "I walked past the intake manifolds on the way in."

"You would have heard the hum."

"It matches the [[Vankh pulse]]'s undertone. Not approximately. Exactly."

[[Augi]] was quiet for a moment. The station moved around them — distant cargo loaders, the vibration of docking clamps, the omnipresent warm bass of the recyclers doing their constant work. And under all of it, the hum. Patient. Precisely calibrated.

"Not because we designed it to," she said at last. "Because whoever designed the station did." She went back to the neural array, her hands resuming their work. "I have tried, on two occasions, to retune the recyclers for higher efficiency. Both times the system self-corrected within a single [[Vankh-Sett]] cycle." A pause. "I am not given to mysticism. I note the fact and move on."

[[Pro]] picked up the pharmaceutical pack and secured it in his tool bag.

"The client whose array that is," he said, looking at the components on the tray. "Are they passing a security screen?"

"Their reasons for needing this work are their own business." She said it the way she said everything: without inflection, without evasion. It was not a rebuke. It was the operating principle of a woman whose entire existence depended on discretion. "Take care of your mother, technician. Come back when the prescription needs renewing."

He was at the door when she spoke again.

"The corridor system runs deeper than the maintenance levels you walked through today. Three sub-levels below the standard access. Same construction signature all the way down." She did not look up from the array. "I mention it because you would eventually find this out yourself, and I prefer you to have the context before you go looking."

[[Pro]] stood in the doorway for a moment.

"Why?"

"Because the people who went looking without context did not know what questions to ask when they found something." She turned a component over with one finger, examining its underside. "You know how to ask questions. It would be a waste."

He stepped back into the maintenance corridor. The door sealed behind him.

The hum was there immediately — warm, present, utterly consistent. He held his scanner to the nearest recycler intake and took a reading. The frequency returned on-screen in numbers that matched, to six decimal places, the [[Vankh pulse]]'s undertone frequency he had logged during transit. Not an approximation. Not a rounding. A match.

He began walking. The unified skeleton surrounded him in every direction — the same alloy, the same seams, the same engineer's hand — carrying the atmosphere that all three factions breathed, running through the skin of a division that had never touched the bone.

His tool bag was heavy with his mother's medicine. The scanner readings sat in his device's memory, patient as everything else he had filed away.

He walked back toward the public concourse, and did not yet let himself ask whose house this was.
